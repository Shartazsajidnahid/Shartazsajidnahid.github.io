Shartaz Sajid Nahid

Prospective PhD Student in Machine Learning

I am a Lecturer in the Department of CSE at East West University. My research interests lie broadly in machine learning, with a particular focus on representation learning, subspace learning, transfer learning, and domain adaptation. Rather than relying primarily on empirical tuning, I am especially interested in developing theoretically grounded machine learning algorithms with mathematical foundations, provable guarantees, and broad applicability across diverse domains.

I received both my B.Sc. and M.Sc. in Software Engineering from the Institute of Information Technology, University of Dhaka. In my master's thesis, I proposed a parameter-free transfer learning method (OCMTL) that learns a shared orthogonal subspace between source and target domains through iterative SVD updates with convergence guarantees. This work was published in a Q1 Elsevier Journal. My current research works are in similar areas, such as, how distribution gap can be addressed, how tensors can be used to learn representations better. I am currently seeking Ph.D. opportunities to further develop my research in machine learning, particularly in similar areas where mathematical analysis can lead to robust and generalizable learning algorithms.

Research Projects

2026 - Present

Transfer learning with subspace and distribution alignment

Proposing by extending the OCMTL framework by incorporating distribution alignment alongside subspace alignment. This approach learns a shared orthogonal subspace while simultaneously aligning the distributions of source and target domains with theoretical guarantees.

2026 - Present

Partial least squares-based transfer learning

Proposing a novel approach based on Partial Least Squares (PLS) to learn shared latent components between source and target domains. Instead of learning all shared components in a single projection matrix, this method selectively identifies and learns the most relevant shared components one by one using deflation process.

2024 - Present

A Low-cost Vision-based System to Improve Efficiency of Assembly Lines in Ready-made Garments Factories

Worked as a research assistant, we developed a framework for recognizing sewing activity on garment assembly lines using color-band markers to track hand movements, building motion trajectories, and classifying activities from trajectory features. Funded by the University Grants Commission, Government of Bangladesh, as part of a UGC-funded project on low-cost vision systems for assembly-line efficiency. Manuscript under review.

Worked as a research assistant, we combined LLM fine-tuning, prompt-based extraction, and traditional NLP methods to pull structured information from unstructured government documents collected across multiple regions.
